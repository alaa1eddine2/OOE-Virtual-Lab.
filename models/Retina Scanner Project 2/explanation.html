Problem 4 — Companion
A security scanner simulates a human eye — using a variable lens to focus on close-up tokens and verify identity through optical imaging.

The mechanical eye works on the same optical principle as the human eye: a lens with variable focal power focuses incoming light onto a fixed detector screen (the "retina").

When a security token is presented, the scanner must accommodate — increasing its optical power to bend light more sharply so the image lands precisely on the sensor.

The entire process happens in microseconds, but it follows strict geometric optics: the thin lens conjugate relation governs every step.

01
Light Capture from the Token

The security token — a card, QR pattern, or biometric marker — is placed at a standard reading distance of OA = −25 cm from the scanner's optical center.

Light reflects off every point on the token's surface. Each point emits a diverging cone of rays that travels toward the mechanical eye's lens.

The scanner cannot process all these rays — it only needs three principal rays per point to construct the full image geometrically.

Key principle: Every point on the object emits light in all directions. The lens collects and converges them back to a single point on the sensor.
02
The Variable Lens

The mechanical eye contains a variable-power lens at its optical center O. Unlike a camera with a fixed lens, this lens can change its focal length by physically deforming — just like the crystalline lens in a human eye.

The lens is described by its optical power P measured in dioptres (δ), defined as P = 1/f′. A higher power means a shorter focal length and more aggressive bending of light.

For a distant object, the relaxed lens has P = 66.67 δ. For a close token, it must increase to accommodate.

The lens is the only moving part. The retina position OA′ = +15 mm is fixed. All focus adjustment happens through lens deformation.
03
Accommodation — Adjusting Power

When the token moves close, diverging rays arrive at a steeper angle. The relaxed lens is not powerful enough — the image would form behind the retina, producing a blurred reading.

The mechanical actuator deforms the lens, increasing its curvature. This raises the optical power from 66.67 δ → 70.67 δ, shortening f′ from 15 mm to 14.15 mm.

The extra power needed is the accommodation amplitude: ΔP = 4.00 δ. This is exactly the effort the lens mechanism must exert.

Formula: 1/f′_acc = 1/OA′ − 1/OA = 1/0.015 − 1/(−0.25) = 66.67 + 4.00 = 70.67 δ
04
Sharp Image Formed on the Retina

With the correct lens power applied, all three principal rays from each object point converge precisely on the retina at OA′ = +15 mm.

The image formed is real, inverted, and reduced — typical of a converging lens with the object beyond the focal point. The sensor records this inverted image and the software corrects for orientation.

Sharpness is measured by how tightly the rays converge. Any deviation in lens power causes a circle of confusion instead of a point — detectable as blur.

The image is real (rays physically cross), inverted (upside-down), and magnified by m = OA′/OA = 0.015/0.25 = 0.06×
05
Signal Processing & Verification

The retina sensor (a CCD or CMOS array) converts the optical image into an electrical signal. Each pixel records light intensity at its position.

The raw signal is fed to a processing unit that checks the token's pattern, geometry, and encoding against a stored database entry.

If the optical power was incorrect and the image is blurry, the contrast drops below threshold and the scanner signals a re-focus before re-reading. Modern scanners complete this loop in under 50 ms.

The scanner uses the optical power value P_acc as a calibration check — it verifies the lens reached the correct power before trusting the image.

Human Eye vs. Mechanical Eye
Human Eye
Biological Lens System
  • Crystalline lens deforms via ciliary muscles
  • Retina = photoreceptors (rods & cones)
  • Typical OA′ ≈ 17 mm (axial length)
  • Accommodation range: ~14 δ (young adult)
  • Responds in ~350 ms
  • Degrades with age (presbyopia)
  • Limited by biological tissue properties
Mechanical Eye
Engineered Optical System
  • Piezoelectric or electrowetting lens actuator
  • Retina = CCD / CMOS pixel array
  • OA′ = 15 mm (fixed by design)
  • Accommodation amplitude: 4.00 δ (this model)
  • Responds in < 50 ms
  • Does not degrade over time
  • Precision limited only by manufacturing tolerance
