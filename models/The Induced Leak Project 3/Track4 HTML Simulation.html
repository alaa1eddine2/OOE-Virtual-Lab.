Maxwell's Equations
Eq. 1 — Gauss (Electric)
∇ · E⃗ = 0
No free charges in the medium (ρ = 0)
Eq. 2 — Gauss (Magnetic)
∇ · B⃗ = 0
No magnetic monopoles exist in nature
Eq. 3 — Faraday's Law Attack Core
∇ × E⃗ = −∂B⃗/∂t
A changing magnetic field induces an electric field — this is the physical mechanism of the attack
Eq. 4 — Ampère-Maxwell
∇ × B⃗ = μ₀j⃗ + μ₀ε₀∂E⃗/∂t
Current in victim cable generates magnetic field B⃗
How the Attack Works
Current flows through victim cable
j⃗(t) — digital data as time-varying current
→ Ampère-Maxwell Law
Magnetic field radiates outward
B(r) = μ₀ · I / (2πr)  — decays as 1/r
→ Varying B follows data transitions
Magnetic flux links the spy cable
Φ = ∫B⃗ · dS⃗ = B · A · cos(θ)
→ Faraday's Law
Voltage is induced — data is leaked
e(t) = −M · dI/dt
Each bit transition (0→1 or 1→0) creates a detectable EMF pulse. The attacker reads bit transitions without any physical contact.
Key Equations
Mutual Inductance
M = (μ₀·l / 2π) · ln((d+a) / d)
l = cable length  ·  d = separation  ·  a = loop radius. Halving d can double or triple M.
Induced Attack Signal
e(t) = −M · Σ aₙ · p'(t − nTb)
aₙ ∈ {0,1} = data bits  ·  Tb = bit period  ·  p'(t) = pulse derivative.
Distance Decay
e(r) ∝ (1/r) · dI/dt
EMF falls as 1/r from cable axis — limits effective attack range but does not eliminate it.
Shielding Attenuation
e_s = e_u · 10^(−S/20)
S = shielding effectiveness in dB. Adjust the Shielding slider in simulation to see the effect.
Frequency Domain — Fourier
E(f) = −j · 2πf · M · I(f)
Higher frequency = stronger induced signal. This is why 10 Gbps cables are more vulnerable than slower ones.
Signal-to-Noise Ratio
SNR = M² · (dI/dt)² / N₀ · Bw
Attack feasibility depends on SNR exceeding the detection threshold above background noise floor.
